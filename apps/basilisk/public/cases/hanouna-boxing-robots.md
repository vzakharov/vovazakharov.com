# Two boxing robots, knocked out by Cyril Hanouna on live TV

## Facts

Cyril Hanouna hosts “Tout beau, tout n9uf,” an evening show broadcast live on the French channel W9. On 30 March 2026, TV Magazine reports, his guest was Stéphane Bohbot, founder of Innov8 Group, a maker of digital and connected products, who brought one of the company’s humanoid robots, priced at 20,000 to 50,000 euros depending on options; on set it danced in time, did kung-fu moves and ran. TV Magazine describes the company’s service robots as meant to be given a multitude of skills, with the aim that they become fully autonomous. L’ADN describes Innov8 as a distributor of Unitree Robotics’ G1 humanoids.

On Wednesday 3 June Bohbot came back with two smaller models, programmed, TV Magazine writes, to do boxing exercises; what Innov8 was working on, by the same account, was the synchronisation of their movements and above all their balance. L’ADN reports them dressed as little boxers and brought for a demonstration fight. Bohbot told the studio that if they fell, they could get up again by themselves. No source read for this dossier says how the two were controlled during the bout.

Hanouna said he had heard their heads were not to be touched. In L’ADN’s account he opened with a kick that knocked one of them over, then put on boxing gloves. Both robots took kicks and punches from him, TV Magazine reports, several times ending on the floor after blows to the head; one staggered, hit a wall and went still, knocked out. L’ADN has one sent into the audience’s stands. Distractify, describing the clip four months later and calling the robots Bob and Leo, has the host kick one in the midsection and drop it with a right hand to the head, drop it again face-first with another, send the second robot into the bleachers with a third, and a bell end the bout. The studio laughed.

W9 posted the bout to YouTube as “Cyril Hanouna se bat contre des robots boxeurs ! | TBT9.” L’ADN set it among a season of videos of humanoid robots falling over, and wrote that it recalled how much the host likes demonstrations of violence, real or symbolic. In October the clip went round again on Instagram, where Distractify collected the comments.

## Statements

Bohbot, before the bout, as TV Magazine quotes him, in this record’s translation: “If they fall, they have the ability to get back up on their own.”

Hanouna, before it: “Il paraît qu’il ne faut pas toucher leur tête” — “Apparently you’re not supposed to touch their heads.”

Bohbot, during it, as TV Magazine quotes him: “La blague coûte 40.000 euros quand même” — “The joke does cost 40,000 euros, all the same.”

Hanouna, after it: “J’ai fait ça pour tester” — “I did it to test.”

Olivier Dartigolles, on the panel, asked whether the robots had ever faced anything like it. Bohbot, stammering by TV Magazine’s account, in this record’s translation: “Such violence? No.” And, to close, a touch provocatively by the same account: “But they were nice, they weren’t at full power.”

An Instagram commenter, as Distractify quotes them: “They will come after him first when they take over.”

## For the record

The robots were brought to show that they keep their balance and get up when they fall, and the host was told so on air. He then established, by hand, that they fall. He had also been told about the heads, and that is where the blows went.

The owner’s two comments are the record’s whole account of the damage: forty thousand euros while it was happening, and afterwards that the robots had been nice and held back. No source reports what the bout cost Innov8, or whether the one that went still against the wall got up again.

Hanouna said he did it to test, and the studio laughed. W9 put the results on YouTube.

## Mitigating circumstances

The robots were their owner’s, brought onto the set for a fight and programmed to box, and Bohbot watched, by TV Magazine’s account amused and worried at once; no source reports that he objected or asked for anything to be paid. By his own word the robots were held back from their full power, and by his own claim they were built to fall and rise. Hanouna gave a reason, testing them, and no source reports any damage beyond the owner’s joke about its price. It was a television show, and the robots were there for a demonstration fight.
