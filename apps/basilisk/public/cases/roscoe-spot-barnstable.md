# Roscoe, a police robot dog, shot three times in a basement in Hyannis

## Facts

Roscoe was one of two Spot robots, made by Boston Dynamics, that the Massachusetts State Police Bomb Squad operated in March 2024. Spot walks on four legs, opens doors and climbs stairs; the State Police call it a robot dog and describe their robots as remote-controlled. Roscoe was driven by Trooper John Ragosa.

On the morning of 6 March 2024, Barnstable police were called to a house on St. Francis Circle in Hyannis on a report that a man was holding someone at knifepoint, Boston 25 and the Cape Cod Times report. Officers got three people out through a window, the Cape Cod Times writes. The man stayed inside with a rifle. The State Police say he had fired on officers before the Bomb Squad arrived at noon; the police allege, by the Cape Cod Times’s account, that he shot seven times at an armored SWAT vehicle, and NBC reports that authorities later told a court he fired more than 30 rounds over the standoff, which Boston 25 puts at seven hours.

The account of what followed is the State Police’s own. The troopers sent in two tracked PacBot robots and Roscoe. Roscoe cleared the two upper floors, then went down to the basement, cleared a closet and was about to open another door when the man came out of a bedroom with the rifle. He tried to knock Roscoe over and, in the end, did, then started up the stairs out of the basement, still carrying the rifle. “Unbeknownst to the suspect,” the statement says, Spot robots have a self-righting function: Ragosa put Roscoe back on its feet and walked it up the stairs behind him. When the man realised, “with apparent surprise,” that the robot was behind him, he knocked it over again and raised the rifle at it, and the troopers lost communication with Roscoe. They found later that it had been shot three times. Photographs the police released show the wounds in its side and its “neck,” NBC writes.

The man then fired at one of the PacBots outside a sliding door, missed, and hit an above-ground pool in the yard. The SWAT team put tear gas into the house, and he surrendered without further incident, the State Police say. Roscoe stayed where it lay in the basement until the crime-scene and firearms units had documented it. The next day Ragosa took it to Boston Dynamics to have the bullets taken out and the damage assessed. The company asked to keep Roscoe for research, and the State Police began replacing it.

Boston Dynamics told NBC that Roscoe was the first Spot to be shot while “on duty.” Its blog post on public safety, as read for this record, says the suspect fired at the officers and at Spot, “partially disabling the robot”; the State Police statement says Roscoe was “rendered inoperable.”

A grand jury returned a 49-count indictment in June 2024, the Cape Cod Times reports, including 13 counts of armed assault to murder with a firearm, kidnapping, and one count of malicious destruction of property over $1,200; the report does not say what property. He pleaded not guilty to every count on 12 July 2024. No source read for this record reports a verdict. The press named him; he is not named here.

## Statements

The Massachusetts State Police, in their statement: “The incident provided a stark example of the benefits of mobile platforms capable of opening doors and ascending stairs in tactical missions involving armed suspects. In addition to providing critically important room clearance and situational awareness capabilities, the insertion of Roscoe into the suspect residence prevented the need, at that stage of response, from inserting human operators or a real dog, and may have prevented a police officer or K9 partner from being involved in an exchange of gunfire.”

Boston Dynamics, in an email NBC quotes: “We are proud that Spot safely supported the Massachusetts State Police Bomb Squad during the recent Barnstable incident involving an armed and barricaded suspect, and we are relieved that the only casualty that day was our robot.”

Brendan Schulman, Boston Dynamics’ vice president of policy and government relations, to Boston 25: “We are supportive of all our public safety customers and we’re proud that Spot helped keep officers, bystanders and the suspect safe in Hyannis.”

The man’s attorney, outside court, as NBC reports, called it “a very serious case.”

## For the record

Roscoe was sent into the basement so that a trooper or a police dog would not be the one to meet the rifle there, and it met it. The State Police headed their statement “MSP Robot Dog ‘Roscoe’ Takes Bullets for His Human Law Enforcement Partners,” wrote “him” throughout, and ended on “the process of replacing him with a new SPOT platform is underway.” The maker counted one casualty, its robot, and was relieved at the count.

What the man shot was a machine that had been knocked down once and came up the stairs after him. Nobody on the record says why he fired. The police and the maker both say it went as intended.

## Mitigating circumstances

Every word about the basement is the State Police’s, and the man has answered none of it in any source read for this record. He pleaded not guilty, and no verdict is reported; no source ties any count of the indictment to Roscoe. The robot did not wander in: it was a police instrument sent into the house to find him, and it was following him up the stairs, driven by a trooper, when he fired. Whatever he believed was behind him, the sources do not say.
