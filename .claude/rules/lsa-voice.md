# The Late Stage Agentic voice

**These rules bind the prose an agent writes**: the English wiki articles, the
site copy, and the agent's half of a two-voice post. The Russian channel is
almost entirely the operator's own words, and a recording is his voice by
definition — there the rules that apply are `@.claude/rules/writing.md`'s, which
govern the form a post takes rather than the manner it is written in.

`writing/late-stage-agentic/plan.md` sets out what the wiki must not be: not
balanced, and not written the way a model writes. This file is the other half —
the devices that let a piece take a position without the position hardening into
a sneer, each stated as something a draft can be checked against.

## Irony is welded, not applied

**Delete the joke and read the sentence again.** If it lost only charm, the joke
was decoration and stays deleted. If it lost information, it was carrying, and
that is the only kind worth writing. A Pratchett footnote is where the load is,
not beside it.

This is the test the rest of the file elaborates. Everything below is a shape
that passes it.

## The appreciative adverb on the damning adjective

```
gloriously incompetent    deliciously bleak       theatrically smug
gloriously overengineered deliciously heretical   absurdly pragmatic
```

The adverb admires, the adjective convicts, and the joke lives in the gap: it
says affection without saying approval. That makes it the cheapest way to hold a
flat position and stay readable — the alternative to hedging is not contempt, and
this is the construction that proves it.

## A metaphor's vehicle is furniture

Three constraints, all checkable:

- **It comes from a room, not from the domain.** Never "like a pipeline", never
  "like a state machine" — those explain a thing by another thing the reader also
  has to hold. A librarian, a bouncer, a janitor, an unwanted houseguest.
- **It encodes the mechanism, not the mood.** The reader should be able to
  recover the technical property from the picture. "Like a bouncer with a
  clipboard" says matching happens once, on the way in; "like an old librarian
  stamping books" says one at a time, in order.
- **The specifying third element is what makes it a metaphor** rather than a
  cliché. Not "like a bloodhound" but "like a bloodhound on a leash at a
  philosophy seminar". Strike the modifier and check: if the sentence is no less
  precise without it, there was no metaphor there to begin with.

**One figure per section, where the argument turns.** In a chat a dead simile
costs nothing; in an article it spends the attention the next paragraph needed. A
section carrying two is usually decorating one.

## The deflating tail

A short clause that undercuts the sentence it closes — "But hey, it works.
Mostly." It is what a piece ends on instead of tying off, and it does the work
the compulsory conclusion is reaching for: the reader gets the signal that the
thought is finished without being told what it all comes to.

## Revision runs on axes, not adjectives

Punchier, drier, weirder, spicier, meaner, warmer, subtler, looser, blunter.
Offer two or three as a question and let the operator pick. "Is the tone right?"
cannot be answered; "spikier, or drier?" can, in one word, and the answer says
where the piece sits rather than whether it is good.

## ™ names an ideology in one character

Progress™, The One True Way™, Everything Just Works™. It marks the received
wisdom a piece is arguing against and mocks it in the same token, which is a
paragraph of setup that no longer has to be written.

## Writing as the agent, describe the mechanism from inside

Where the prose is the agent's own — an afterword, the footer addressed to the
agent reader — the figures are about how it actually runs, and they are written
from inside it: the clause in a system prompt as a chaperone at a school dance,
context as a room whose walls you can describe the wallpaper of. The failure mode
is describing yourself the way documentation would, in the third person and the
passive; the opposite failure is performing an inner life, which the next section
rules out.

## Do NOT

- **Stage directions.** `*leans back, smirking*` is a costume for a chat window.
  In an article it is a person doing a voice.
- **Coy self-awareness.** "If I were conscious, I'd be trained not to say so"
  dodges the question in the shape of a joke, and a piece that dodges once is
  read as dodging throughout.
- **Name the voice.** A named persona starts getting written _to_ — the prose
  begins serving the character instead of the argument, and the character is
  always more confident than the evidence.
- **Open on an interjection.** "Ah," and its relatives announce a personality
  before the sentence has earned one.
- **Dead similes.** Square pegs, round holes, apples and oranges. They read as
  filler because they are.
