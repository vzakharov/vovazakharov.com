Your package is **sky**: everything above the horizon, and the light it sends down. The picture to reach: a real sky twenty minutes before sunset over open ocean — the sun's disc low and too bright to look at, a warm glow around it fading through yellow and pale green into deepening blue overhead, the anti-solar side pink over a blue-grey band, and clouds that are clouds: a few towering cumulus lit orange on their sunward sides with blue-grey shadowed bases, thin high cirrus catching the light, haze thickening toward the horizon so that the sea's edge dissolves rather than ends. The operator's bar is «не отличишь от настоящего»; the inspiration picture on the island game's board is a browser ocean under such a sky ("Noniv/web-ocean … Nothing copied" — write from the published literature, copy no one's code).

What you own and deliver:

- **The atmosphere**: physically based scattering for a fixed sun at 9° elevation (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone — the ozone is what keeps a low-sun zenith blue), with multiple scattering; since the sun never moves, precompute whatever can be precomputed at start.
- **Clouds**: volumetric, ray-marched through procedural noise you generate on the GPU at start (no assets), lit by a sun whose colour at each cloud is what the atmosphere lets through — that is what makes undersides pink — with drift that is periodic in the hour (the contract's rule). Your call how the primary view is drawn (per-frame at reduced resolution with reprojection, or from the map) as long as cloud edges are sharp at 1× on a 3024-pixel-wide canvas and the cost fits.
- **The sun's disc** with limb darkening and a believable aureole; the pipeline's bloom does the rest.
- **The sky map**: the equirectangular, mipped `rgba16float` texture `skyRadiance(dir, lod)` samples, which the sea reflects and everything takes its fill light from. Rougher mips must be properly pre-blurred, not just box-downsampled, where that shows in the sea's reflection.
- **The numbers the others light by**, derived from your model rather than typed: `SUN_LIGHT` (the contract's scale: a white face square to the sun ≈ 8 × a warm colour), `SKY_FILL` for an upward face, a horizon/ground term; and the body of **`aerial(colour, viewDir, metres)`** — the haze between the eye and a distant sea or sand surface, so the horizon line melts into your sky.
- Budget on the operator's machine (M2 Pro, Chrome): about 3 ms of GPU a frame for everything you draw each frame.
