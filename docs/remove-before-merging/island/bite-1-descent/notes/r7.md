## 1. Phone orbit dashed lines — cause found, not fixed

Diagnostic build (scratch only): globe red, background green, land material
mixed 60 % magenta. The dashed rectangles turned **magenta**: they are the
**land material**, not the globe and not the sky. They sit on tile edges in
open sea around the island, only where a tile carries a land mesh (tiles with
any ground ≥ 0); no line crosses the land itself.

Reading: sub-pixel pinholes along T-junctions of the **sea** surface (the
sea's skirts hang vertical, so seen from overhead they are edge-on and cover
no pixel) show the land tile's own sea floor just below. Floor within ~20 m of
the surface is coloured sand-to-sea (`LAND_STOPS`) and lit with the land's
brighter sun (2.7 vs 1.58), hence pale. Unverified which: the floor of the
land grid itself vs its skirt — next diagnostic: colour land skirts (heights
attribute index ≥ grid²) separately.

Fix candidates, at the cause: (a) skirts that flare outward as they drop
(bottom ring pushed out ~one cell as well as down), so from above they
overlap the neighbour and fill pinholes, in `fillTile`; (b) don't draw the
land mesh's floor where the sea covers it deeply — not enough, the shallow
band is exactly what shows. (a) is preferred; it also fills the desktop
1 000 km dark ticks if those are pinholes onto the globe (not yet checked —
crop of `diag1/desktop-1000km.png` at x 420–540, y 20–110 would say: red =
globe).

## 2. 10 km soft — design ready, half written

Measured (`tmp/r7/measure.ts`, eye over (0,0), real coast, frustum on):

| height | desktop tiles | phone tiles | nadir `detailCut` | nadir leaf 2·spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| orbit | 60 | 60 | 128 km | 128 km (level 3) |
| 1000 km | 45 | 68 | 62.3 km | 16 km (level 6, coast-finer) |
| 10 km | 26 | 32 | 448 m | 250 m (level 12) |
| 100 m | 103 | 89 | 3.9 m | 3.9 m (level 18) |
| 2 m | 86 | 76 | 3.9 m | 3.9 m |

So the cut wastes ~1 octave at 10 km and 2 at 1 000 km (coast-finer tiles).

Design (exact along shared edges, no selection sent to the worker):
`detailCut(eye, coast?)(x, y) = max over the *unbalanced* leaves L of the
split rule near (x, y) of 2·sp(L)·max(0, 1 − dist((x,y), L)/(side_L/2))`.
It is a pure function of (eye, coast, point), so every tile computes the
same value at a shared vertex; it is ≥ 2·spacing of the selected tile there,
because balance and culling only ever make the selection finer than the
unbalanced leaf; it ramps continuously (halving over a quarter of the coarse
leaf's side). Evaluate by descending from the root into nodes within
`side_N/2` of the point, pruning nodes whose 2·sp ≤ the best so far; memoise
the split decision per tile id within one cut closure.

Written: `model/split.ts` — `viewDistance` and `splitRule(eye, coast?)`
moved out of `detail.ts`/`quadtree.ts` (not yet wired: `quadtree.ts` still
has its own copy; `detail.ts` still exports `viewDistance`). Left: quadtree
uses `splitRule`; `detailCut` as above; worker passes `field`
(`detailCut(eye, built)`); `detail.test.ts` keeps passing (it calls
`detailCut(eye)` with no coast, matching `selectTiles(eye)` with none) plus
a test that the nadir cut equals the nadir leaf's 2·spacing.

Beyond the cut: the grid itself is coarse at 10 km — a 125 m cell is ~40 px on
desktop. If the frame is still soft after the cut, `TILE_GRID` 65 (same tile
counts, 4× fill time) or `SPLIT_RATIO` 0.5 (~4× tiles) is the lever; the
operator's call.
