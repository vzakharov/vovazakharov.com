# R6 — a clean coastline

## Done

1. Smoothing (`entities/coast/model/smooth.ts`, exported `smoothCoast`,
   `SmoothOptions`, and `signedArea`): drop rings under `minArea`, resample each
   ring at `resampleStep`, Gaussian blur along the ring (`sigma`), D–P at
   `tolerance`; the blur halves per ring until the result keeps its turning, is
   simple and crosses no ring already smoothed. Largest first. Script runs it at
   `minArea 60, step 1, sigma 3, tolerance 0.3` px. 7 tests in
   `smooth.test.ts`.
2. `coast.json` regenerated: 25 rings, 1 356 vertices, 6.2109 km/px (was
   6.2009: the main body's bbox narrowed a little), 38 KB. **No ring dropped**:
   the tracer's own `islandFloor` (60 px²) already removes specks; the smallest
   kept ring is 239 px² (~9 200 km²) and every kept ring is an island inked on
   the drawing. Overlay regenerated in `frames/coast-overlay.png`.
3. `Simplified` (`{ tolerance }`) base type in `ring.ts`, shared by
   `TraceOptions` and `SmoothOptions` (type-overlap).

## Finding

At orbit the tiles are level 3: 2 048 km a side, 32 cells, so **64 km between
vertices**. Apatin, the northern fingers and Korump's arm are 10–20 km wide on
the plane; the grid samples them on and off, which is what frays them into
specks at orbit. Smoothing the polygon cannot fix that; the vertex density at
orbit is the terrain's (R7's paths).

## Left (paused at the subagent context budget)

1. Shader, not started. Why the land's underwater colour stops cannot carry
   the band: the sea mesh at height 0 is nearer the eye than any ground under
   it, so it hides every underwater fragment except on depth ties. And the sea
   floor falls ~115 m in the first km (`height.ts` shelf), while a pixel at
   orbit is ~26 km, so a band defined in metres of depth is sub-pixel there.
   Designed, not written — sea material reads the scene's depth:
   - `sea.transparent = true` (renders after the opaque land; three's default
     `LessEqual` still lets the sea win skirt ties, keeping F's fix).
   - `ground = logarithmicDepthToViewZ(viewportDepthTexture().x, cameraNear, cameraFar)`
     (the renderer has `logarithmicDepthBuffer`; NodeMaterial writes
     `viewZToLogarithmicDepth`, three 0.187), all from `three/tsl`.
   - `thickness = max(0, (positionView.z - ground) * length(positionView) / -positionView.z)`
     — water along the ray.
   - `pixel = fwidth(thickness)`; `opacityNode = smoothstep(0, 1.5·pixel, thickness)`
     (the antialiased edge: under the thin edge the floor near 0 m is sand, so
     land-above and sea-at-edge meet in one colour);
     `shallow = 1 - smoothstep(0, clamp(3·pixel, SHALLOW_MIN, SHALLOW_MAX), thickness)`,
     `colorNode = mix(SEA, SHALLOW_COLOUR (~0x1f6f8f), shallow)`. The clamp keeps
     depth discontinuities (sky behind) from lighting up the band.
   - The globe shares the sea material, so it turns transparent too: it is not
     in the depth copy, so thickness behind it reads far → deep, as wanted.
   - Unverified: depth copy with `antialias: true` on the WebGL2 fallback.
2. Known limit of that design: `tile-worker.ts` gives a tile no land mesh when
   its highest vertex is under 0 (`land: high < 0 ? null : ground`), so where
   the band runs into such a tile there is no floor behind the sea and the band
   is cut at the tile edge. Fix belongs to the worker (not R6's paths): keep
   land for tiles whose high is above the band's depth.
3. Play run, desktop and phone, five heights; orbit and 1 000 km frames into
   `notes/r6/`.
