# Package c2 — fireflies circle their hosts: hand-over note

c.md's first "Left" bullet. Paths under `src/pages/mushrooms/`.

## Done

- **Cause**: none of c.md's three suspects. A throwaway probe on the tabL
  `dusk` play printed each firefly's host and seat: the hosts are the
  nearest caps and flowers, sorted correctly by `distance`, every one
  drawn, and `Seat.drawn` sits on the cap's top or the flower's head in
  the container's coordinates. The ring was the fault. `model/firefly.ts`
  measured it in the firefly's unit, which is the insects' size (60 CSS px
  on tabL at the clump's distance), with a radius of 1–1.7 and a lift of
  0.9–1.8. That put each firefly 60–100 px to the side of its host and
  55–110 px above it. A flower head is ~25 px across, so they ended up in
  open grass and up toward the brow.
- **Fix**: `RING` 0.45–0.8, `LIFT` 0.3–0.55, `FLAT` 0.2–0.35, which keeps
  the ring's lowest point above the host (the existing "over the host"
  test). New test in `model/firefly.test.ts`: every firefly of the dozen
  stays within 0.8 units across and 0.9 units up of its host.
- **Looked at**: `frames/bite-17/c2-fireflies-tabL.png`, the tabL `dusk-dusk`
  frame. Fireflies ring the two caps and the closed flowers nearest the
  eye, at most two to a host.

## Left

- Nothing on this bullet. Two fireflies sharing a host can overlap
  their halos when their phases line up. That is cosmetic, and `hostFor`
  already spreads them across `NEAREST`.

## Decided

- Rings stay sized in the insects' unit, not in each host's own size: a
  cap is wider than a flower's head, so a firefly sits nearer a cap's top
  than its edge. That still reads as circling, and it needs no host
  size in `Seat`.
