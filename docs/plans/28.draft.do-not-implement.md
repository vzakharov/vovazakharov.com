# #28 — Re-window the before/after rates on the commit-cumsum chart

Issue export: `docs/issue/28/issue.md`. Closes #28.

The chart reports the switch into the cloud as **6.2 → 8.2 units of work per day**. Both windows are drawn wrong: the "before" window starts inside a docs-only phase, and the "after" window runs four months past the point where the job changed, over calendar days that include every weekend and a week off. Pick defensible window boundaries, recompute both rates over working days, draw the windows on the chart, and bring the prose and alt text into line.

## Where the dates come from

**The Playgram repository, not the picture.** `Playgramai/playgramapp` is cloned to `tmp/playgramapp` (`gh repo clone … -- --filter=blob:none --no-checkout --single-branch --branch main`) and every date below is read off its commit history. The committed dataset — `public/case-studies/assets/playgram-commit-cumsum.csv` — cannot answer the questions the issue asks, because documentation-only commits are already filtered out of it: a docs-only *phase* looks identical to a holiday there.

Classifying a commit as documentation-only when every path it touches is `*.md`, `docs/`, `.claude/` or `bubble/` (the Bubble export dump) reproduces the chart's headline exactly — **1,287 code commits, 6 Mar – 21 Aug 2026**.

### The docs-only phase ends on 11 March

Code commits against documentation commits, per day, from the repository:

| Date | Day | Code | Docs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fri 6 Mar | 0 | 3 | 13 |
| Sat 7 Mar | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| Sun 8 Mar | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| Mon 9 Mar | 3 | 0 | 12 |
| Tue 10 Mar | 4 | 1 | 22 |

Documentation is the work through 10 March and stops being the work on 11 March. The article's own timeline table already says as much — "6 Mar · First commit. Not code — a splitting script and a pile of research" — so the date is consistent with the prose, not invented for the chart.

### The May vacation: 6–10 May

The switch into the cloud falls on **Saturday 25 April** (4 commits that day, none on the Sunday). Work then runs hard — including the weekend of 2–3 May — before stopping:

| Date | Code commits |
| --- | --- |
| Tue 5 May | 16 |
| Wed 6 May | 3 |
| Thu 7 May – Sun 10 May | 0, 1, 0, 0 |
| **Mon 11 May** | **10** |

The "after" window opens on the first full working day in the cloud, **Monday 27 April**, and the vacation falls inside it, cut out as a named absence — see "What counts as a working day" below.

### `4.1.0` is 24 June

Confirmed against the repository's tags (`4.1.0 → 2026-06-24`). Before it the job was shipping the Bubble-parity app; after it, bug fixes and new features. The chart already plots the `4.1.0` dot at that date, so the window's right edge lands exactly on an existing mark.

## What counts as a working day

**Any day with a code commit on it.** No productivity threshold: a light day is still a day at work, and filtering days by how much landed on them would be measuring the thing the rate is supposed to report.

**The one exclusion is the May vacation, 6–10 May** — excluded because it is a known absence, not because it was slow. That distinction is the whole rule. A holiday is not a bad day at work; it is not a day at work.

Where the vacation's edges fall is what the commit counts are for. 6 May carries 3 and 8 May carries 1, bracketed by zeros, inside a stretch the author remembers as time off — and against a daily distribution where 63 days sit at ten or more commits and only a thin 25-day tail spreads across one to four, a 3 and a 1 read as something trickling in from a laptop, not as two days of work.

**This is not a filter applied to one window and withheld from the other.** Inside 27 April – 24 June there are no low-commit days outside the vacation at all, so a threshold applied there as well would remove nothing further. The before window keeps its light days (14, 22 and 24 March, 13 and 23 April) because they are light days, not absences — see the open question for the one place that choice is arguable.

## The dates and the two rates

| | Window | Calendar days | Working days | Commits | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Before** | 11 Mar – 24 Apr | 45 | 34 | 307 | **9.0 / working day** |
| **After** | 27 Apr – 24 Jun, less 6–10 May | 54 | 42 | 607 | **14.5 / working day** |

Unmeasured in either window: the switch weekend (25–26 April) and the vacation.

**The effect is larger than the published figure says.** 6.2 → 8.2 is a factor of 1.32; **9.0 → 14.5 is 1.60** — three fifths again as much work per working day, not a third more.

### The weekly panel says the same thing, which is the check on it

Weekly totals go from a median 44 to a median 74, a factor of 1.68 — and that arrives from an independent direction, so it is worth showing that the two reconcile:

- **×1.60** — more work per working day
- **×1.03** — marginally more working days per week (5.29 before, 5.44 after)
- **×1.65** compounded, against the ×1.68 the weekly medians show

So essentially **all** of the weekly gain is speed rather than more days at the desk. That is a stronger claim than the raw weekly figure alone, and it is the sentence the article should carry: the days did not get longer or more numerous, the work per day changed.

### Why the pre-vacation sprint looks steeper than it is

27 April – 5 May rises faster on the page than anything after it, and the chart is not lying: all nine of those calendar days were worked, the weekend of 2–3 May included, in a sprint at the 6 May deadline. Calendar slope and working-day rate coincide there at **12.7** — the only stretch where they do, because it contains no days off of any kind. The post-vacation run is flatter on the page (11.0 per calendar day) and faster per day actually worked (**14.9**), because seven weekends sit inside it.

Worth keeping straight when reading the redrawn chart: the bracket spans calendar time, the label reports working days, and the two disagree most exactly where the eye is most confident.

### The counts come from the committed CSV

The repository supplies the **dates** (which only it can); the CSV supplies the **counts**.

The chart's curve is drawn from the CSV, and a rate label printed beside a curve has to be the slope of *that* curve or the picture and the number disagree — which is the defect this issue is filed about. The two datasets are close but not identical (totals agree at 1,287, mean absolute difference 0.95 commits/day); the divergence is adjustments made when the chart was prepared, not rewritten history, and is not worth reopening. Regenerating the dataset and redrawing the curve from a fresh clone was considered and rejected: it moves the picture rather than the annotation, and needs a chart generator this repo does not have.

**The after-bracket draws as two segments** — 287.00–323.12 and 344.38–533.50 — with the vacation between them, carrying a small `.an2` "days off" beneath the gap. The gap's edges sit on day boundaries (half a day either side of 6–10 May) rather than on the 6 May and 10 May marks themselves, so it covers the days removed rather than ending inside them. This is the shape of the annotation on the issue: two segments bracketing the after-window with a days-off gap between them.

The vacation is the only gap, and the only exclusion that needs one: it is the sole named absence, and it sits under the visible plateau in the curve, which would otherwise read as something the window was counting. Weekends come out of the denominator too, but at 4.25px each they would be noise on a bracket 250px long — the `working day` in the label is what states that rule.

The short bare stretch between the two brackets' inner ends (274.25 – 287.00) is the switch weekend, measured in neither window. It is too narrow to label; the existing "into the cloud" rule at 278.5 falls inside it and explains it.

**1. The two weekend trickle days in the "before" window.**

14 March (Sat) and 22 March (Sun) carry one commit each and count as full working days, which is the same shape as 8 May inside the vacation — a weekend with something trickling in. Under the rule as specified they stay, because they are not part of any named absence; a 0-commit Saturday is out of the denominator and a 1-commit Saturday is in it, which is the one place the line is hard to defend.

**This one runs against the story, so it is worth deciding rather than inheriting.** Keeping them *raises* the ratio, by inflating the before window's denominator:

- **(a) Keep them (recommended — the rule as you specified it).** 9.0 → 14.5, ratio **1.60**.
- (b) Treat a weekend day with fewer than five commits as a day off, everywhere. Only these two days change hands — 2–3 May was a properly worked weekend and stays. 9.5 → 14.5, ratio **1.52**.

Recommending (a) because it is what you asked for and the effect is small, but flagging it because it is the only remaining choice that makes the headline bigger rather than smaller.

## DRY notes

- **The alt text stays triplicated across the three cuts.** It is identical in `playgram.md`, `playgram.mini.md` and `playgram.nano.md`, and it is tempting to extract. Don't: the content pipeline reads each markdown file as an independent published artifact served raw at its own URL (`.claude/rules/content.md`), so a shared fragment would need an include mechanism the pipeline does not have and would break the property that the `.md` in the repo, on GitHub and on the site are the same bytes. Three coordinated edits is the correct cost.
- **The window dates are stated once per surface, and the surfaces are genuinely different audiences** — the SVG's visible labels, the SVG's `<desc>`, and the prose in two cuts. There is no shared runtime between an SVG served as an image and markdown compiled at build time, so a single source of truth for them would mean generating the SVG, which is the rejected regenerate-everything option, not a refactor.
- **No new helper, module or type is added.** The change is text and geometry inside one committed SVG plus prose in three markdown files. The x-scale arithmetic (`66 + day × 4.25`) is applied once, by hand, to six marks — extracting a function for six literals in a static asset would be worse than the literals.
- **The analysis scripts stay in `tmp/`.** The per-day classification used to pick the dates is throwaway: it reads a clone that is not part of this repository and cannot run in CI, and the dataset it would generate is explicitly not being adopted.
