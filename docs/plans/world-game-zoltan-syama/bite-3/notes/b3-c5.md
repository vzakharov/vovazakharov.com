# b3-c5 — Earth in the game

Branch `wt/b3-c5`, base `c606e694`. Step C5 of `b3-c-map.md` § 7. The shots use
the envelope already on the branch: `PROBE_ENVELOPE` (6, 2), which matters only
for #2–#6 because #0 is pinned. C4 has not landed yet.

## What landed

- `scripts/lib/shot-sets.ts`: `shotsFor(world)`. #1 keeps its five heights and
  the inlet. #0 gets orbit over 0°, 90° E, 180° and 90° W on the equator, plus
  both poles, and 1,000 km over Italy (41 N 14 E), Florida (27 N 81 W) and Japan
  (36 N 138 E). #2–#6 get orbit over 0° and 180°.
- Hours come from the hook alone. Hour 0 is the opening's afternoon, and the
  planet turns 15° an hour, so meridian `L` reaches that same sun at
  `(opening.lon − L) / 15` hours. Coast frames use that hour, so they get the
  opening's low sun for relief. Orbit frames are shot 2.5 h earlier. At the
  opening's hour the sun stands 64° west on #0 and the eastern third of the disc
  is night; 2.5 h earlier lights everything but the rim.
- Poles: the equinox sun grazes both poles at any hour, so the hour only decides
  which half of the disc is lit. North uses meridian −15°, which lights North
  America, Greenland and Europe and leaves Siberia dark. South uses meridian
  30°, which lights the Peninsula/Weddell/Atlantic half.
- `play-island.ts` calls `setTime` before each shot that names an hour. It also
  drops an import of `Degrees` from `./lib/azimuthal`, which no longer exports
  it.

## Verdict: Earth reads as Earth

All frames are in `b3-c5-play/`, named `world0-<screen>-<shot>.png`.

- **Continents placed and shaped right; east not mirrored.** `orbit-0` shows
  Africa centred, Europe and the Mediterranean above it, and South America at
  the left edge. `orbit-90e` shows Arabia, India and Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia,
  Sumatra, Java, Borneo and Australia at lower right. `orbit-180` shows the
  Pacific with Australia, New Guinea and New Zealand at lower left, Japan upper
  left and Hawaii a speck right of centre. `orbit-90w` shows the Americas, the
  Gulf, the Caribbean and an intact Central American isthmus. From above the
  north pole, longitude grows counter-clockwise (Canada → Greenland → Europe →
  Asia), and from below the south pole it grows clockwise (South America →
  Africa → Australia → New Zealand). Both are correct.
- **Poles not iced.** This is the clearest miss. Greenland (`orbit-north`) is
  green and brown with one white spot. Antarctica (`orbit-south`) is brown with
  scattered white patches. Land colour follows the synthetic height, so the ice
  sheets get no ice colour. The outlines are right: Greenland, the Canadian
  Arctic islands, Hudson Bay, Scandinavia, and Antarctica with the Peninsula and
  the Ross/Weddell indents. Half of each pole frame is night, which an equinox
  sun makes unavoidable.
- **Coasts recognisable at 1,000 km.**
  - **Italy**: the boot, heel and Gargano spur, Istria, Corsica and Sardinia on
    both screens. Sicily and Tunisia show on phone; desktop's 1440 × 900 frame
    ends above Sicily.
  - **Florida**: the peninsula, Lake Okeechobee as a lake and the Georgia coast.
    Phone adds Cuba, the Isle of Youth, Andros, Chesapeake and Lakes
    Erie/Ontario. The Florida Keys are missing.
  - **Japan**: Honshu with Noto and Boso, Shikoku, Kyushu's edge, Hokkaido,
    Sakhalin and Korea. Phone adds Lake Khanka. Sado is missing, and Honshu is a
    little fat.
- **Defects, reported and not fixed:**
  1. Highland white patches sit in the wrong places. The Sahara gets about ten
     snow-white blobs a few hundred km across (`orbit-0`). Amazonia gets a large
     white patch (`orbit-90w`), where the land is lowland in reality. The same
     patches appear inland of the US Gulf coast (`florida`) and in the
     Balkans/Alps band (`italy`). The heights are synthetic and are not Earth's
     relief.
  2. A spurious pentagonal sand islet sits in the Gulf of Mexico at about 26.5 N
     88.5 W, on the left edge of `world0-desktop-florida.png`. A faint blue spot
     sits near the Keys (phone, below the Florida tip).
  3. Orbit frames show a fine moiré or dither texture over land, most visible on
     Africa and Asia.
