# #28 — Re-window the before/after rates on the commit-cumsum chart

Issue export: `docs/issue/28/issue.md`. Closes #28.

The chart reports the switch into the cloud as **6.2 → 8.2 units of work per day**. Both windows are drawn wrong: the "before" window starts inside a docs-only phase, and the "after" window runs four months past the point where the job changed, over calendar days that include every weekend and a week off. Pick defensible window boundaries, recompute both rates over the re-windowed stretches, draw the windows on the chart, and bring the prose and alt text into line.

## Where the dates come from

**The Playgram repository, not the picture.** `Playgramai/playgramapp` is cloned to `tmp/playgramapp` (`gh repo clone … -- --filter=blob:none --no-checkout --single-branch --branch main`) and every date below is read off its commit history. The committed dataset — `public/case-studies/assets/playgram-commit-cumsum.csv` — cannot answer the questions the issue asks, because documentation-only commits are already filtered out of it: a docs-only *phase* looks identical to a holiday there.

Classifying a commit as documentation-only when every path it touches is `*.md`, `docs/`, `.claude/` or `bubble/` (the Bubble export dump) reproduces the chart's headline exactly — **1,287 code commits, 6 Mar – 21 Aug 2026**.

Documentation is the work through 10 March and stops being the work on 11 March. The article's own timeline table already says as much — "6 Mar · First commit. Not code — a splitting script and a pile of research" — so the date is consistent with the prose, not invented for the chart.

### The May vacation: 6–10 May

The switch into the cloud falls on **Saturday 25 April** (4 commits that day, none on the Sunday). Work then runs hard — including the weekend of 2–3 May — before stopping:

The "after" window opens on the first full working day in the cloud, **Monday 27 April**, and the vacation falls inside it, cut out as a named absence — see "Calendar days, not working days" below.

### `4.1.0` is 24 June

Confirmed against the repository's tags (`4.1.0 → 2026-06-24`). Before it the job was shipping the Bubble-parity app; after it, bug fixes and new features. The chart already plots the `4.1.0` dot at that date, so the window's right edge lands exactly on an existing mark.

## Calendar days, not working days

The issue asks for the rates over "worked days, not calendar days". **This plan keeps calendar days** — a deliberate departure, for three reasons.

**The re-windowing already fixes what the worked-days rule was there to fix.** The issue's objection is that idle days drag the rate down without saying anything about how fast the work went. That was true of the published figure, whose "after" window ran 118 days across a vacation and a change of job. It is not true of two comparable windows: 34 working days in 45 before, 42 in 54 after — 75.6% against 77.8%. The weekends sit in both windows in the same proportion, so they cancel out of the comparison rather than distorting it. Switching basis moves the ratio from 1.65 to 1.60; it is not carrying the correction.

**A calendar-day label is the slope of the line the reader is looking at.** The x-axis is calendar time. Print 11.2 beside the bracket and it is exactly what the bracketed segments rise at; print 14.5 and the reader's eye measures 11.2 from the picture. The issue's own closing requirement is that the picture and the number agree, and only one basis delivers that.

**It keeps the published unit.** 6.2 and 8.2 were per calendar day, so 6.8 and 11.2 are the same measurement re-windowed, not a different measurement wearing the old one's clothes. Nobody has to be told the units changed.

It also dissolves every weekend edge case — whether a Saturday is a working day, whether a one-commit Sunday counts, what to do with the properly-worked weekend of 2–3 May. On a calendar basis none of those questions arise.

**The one exclusion is the May vacation, 6–10 May** — excluded because it is a known absence, not because it was quiet. A holiday is not a bad day at work; it is not a day at work. That is the single adjustment either window gets, and the chart draws it as a gap so the reader can see it being made.

The commit counts locate the vacation's edges and do nothing else. 6 May carries 3 and 8 May carries 1, bracketed by zeros, in a stretch the author remembers as time off; against a distribution where 63 days sit at ten or more commits and a thin 25-day tail spreads across one to four, a 3 and a 1 read as something trickling in from a laptop rather than as two days of work.

## The dates and the two rates

| | Window | Calendar days | Commits | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Before** | 11 Mar – 24 Apr | 45 | 307 | **6.8 units/day** |
| **After** | 27 Apr – 24 Jun, less 6–10 May | 54 | 607 | **11.2 units/day** |

Unmeasured in either window: the switch weekend (25–26 April) and the vacation.

**The effect is larger than the published figure says.** 6.2 → 8.2 is a factor of 1.34; **6.8 → 11.2 is 1.65** — two thirds again as much work per day, not a third more.

### The weekly panel is the independent check, and it agrees

Weekly totals go from a median 44 to a median 74, a factor of **1.68**, against the upper panel's 1.65. Two measures, different denominators, arriving within two per cent of each other — which is why the lower panel's subtitle can say "up by about two thirds" and mean the same thing the upper panel means, rather than quietly reporting a second ratio.

### The pre-vacation sprint really is the steepest stretch on the chart

27 April – 5 May rises faster than anything after it, and it is not an artefact: all nine of those days were worked, the weekend of 2–3 May included, in a sprint at the 6 May deadline. It runs at **12.7 a day** against the post-vacation run's **11.0**, and the window's 11.2 sits between them, closer to the longer stretch.

Both segments are inside the measured window and both are drawn under the bracket, so the average is visibly an average of what is on the page. Nothing here needs explaining away — on a calendar basis, the eye and the arithmetic agree.

### The counts come from the committed CSV

The repository supplies the **dates** (which only it can); the CSV supplies the **counts**.

The chart's curve is drawn from the CSV, and a rate label printed beside a curve has to be the slope of *that* curve or the picture and the number disagree — which is the defect this issue is filed about. The two datasets are close but not identical (totals agree at 1,287, mean absolute difference 0.95 commits/day); the divergence is adjustments made when the chart was prepared, not rewritten history, and is not worth reopening. Regenerating the dataset and redrawing the curve from a fresh clone was considered and rejected: it moves the picture rather than the annotation, and needs a chart generator this repo does not have.

## What changes

### 1. `public/case-studies/assets/playgram-commit-cumsum.svg`

The curve, bars, dots and milestone leaders are untouched. What changes is the annotation layer.

**Draw the two windows.** The upper panel's x-scale is `x = 66 + day × 4.25`, day 0 = 6 March, so:

| Mark | Date | x |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before-window start | 11 Mar | 87.25 |
| Before-window end | 24 Apr | 274.25 |
| After-window start | 27 Apr | 287.00 |
| Vacation gap, left edge | 5/6 May boundary | 323.12 |
| Vacation gap, right edge | 10/11 May boundary | 344.38 |
| After-window end | 24 Jun | 533.50 |

Add a measurement bracket per window in the band below the upper panel's zero gridline (`y = 402`), around `y = 414`: a `.rule` line across the span with short upward end ticks. The right end lands on 533.50, directly under the existing `4.1.0` dot.

**The after-bracket draws as two segments** — 287.00–323.12 and 344.38–533.50 — with the vacation between them, carrying a small `.an2` "days off" beneath the gap. The gap's edges sit on day boundaries (half a day either side of 6–10 May) rather than on the 6 May and 10 May marks themselves, so it covers the days removed rather than ending inside them. This is the shape of the annotation on the issue: two segments bracketing the after-window with a days-off gap between them.

The vacation is the only gap because it is the only exclusion: it sits under the visible plateau in the curve, which would otherwise read as something the window was counting. Nothing else is excluded, so nothing else needs a gap.

The short bare stretch between the two brackets' inner ends (274.25 – 287.00) is the switch weekend, measured in neither window. It is too narrow to label; the existing "into the cloud" rule at 278.5 falls inside it and explains it.

**Retarget the slope labels.** They currently float free; centre each over its own bracket, and carry the window in the sub-label so the reader can check the claim:

- `6.8 units/day` · `three agents, one screen · 11 Mar – 24 Apr`
- `11.2 units/day` · `twenty agents, reviewed as pull requests · 27 Apr – 24 Jun`

Centre the after-label over the whole bracket including its gap, not over either segment.

**Update the weekly panel's subtitle** (currently "up by about a third after the switch") to the two thirds the weekly medians show — the same two thirds the upper panel now reports, so the two panels read as one claim.

**Rewrite `<desc>`.** It quotes 6.2 and 8.2 and attributes the change to 25 April; it must name the two windows and the per-working-day basis instead. `<desc>` is the only description a reader who cannot see the image gets, so it carries the same window dates.

Angle brackets stay out of the `<style>` block — an SVG is parsed as XML (`.claude/rules/content.md`).

### 2. The three markdown cuts

All three share one alt text, quoting 6.2 and 8.2:

- `public/case-studies/playgram.md:89` — alt text
- `public/case-studies/playgram.mini.md:132` — alt text; `:134` — prose ("units per day go from 6.2 to 8.2 … about a third more of them")
- `public/case-studies/playgram.nano.md:40` — alt text; `:38` — same prose

The alt text is one sentence repeated verbatim in three files; keep it that way (see DRY notes) and change all three together.

**The median unit size becomes "about 380 changed lines", either side.** Both cuts currently spell it as "375 changed lines before, 384 after", which invites the reader to compare two numbers whose whole point is that they do not differ — and which would otherwise need re-deriving over the new windows, since they were computed across the 25 April switch. Rounding both to one figure says what the sentence means, removes the mismatched basis, and needs no recomputation.

**Each rate sentence names the vacation in one clause** — "a week off in May aside", or whatever reads naturally in that cut's register. The vacation is the only adjustment either window gets, and leaving it in would give 10.4 a day and a ratio of 1.52, so it is worth about 8% of the headline. A reader who is going to ask about it deserves to have it said rather than to infer it from a break in a bracket; a clause is enough, and the chart's labelled gap carries the rest.

The timeline table in `playgram.md` (`6 Mar`, `10 Mar`, `25 Apr`, `24 Jun` …) is unaffected — its dates already agree with the repository.

## Settled along the way

Decisions taken during planning, kept as one line each so they are not relitigated in implementation:

- **The after-window opens at the switch, not after the vacation.** Starting on 11 May would throw away eight worked days of cloud-era work to avoid days the vacation cut already removes.
- **The before-window starts 11 March**, where the repository shows code overtaking documentation — not 12 March, the article's "first feature code" row, which would be tuning against a second source.
- **The committed CSV stays.** Regenerating the dataset from a fresh clone moves the picture rather than the annotation, and needs a chart generator this repo does not have.
- **No working-day denominator, and so no threshold.** A five-commit floor was tried as a uniform rule; it lands the ratio at 1.41 and generates weekend edge cases that the calendar basis does not have.
- **The vacation is cut as a named absence, not as slow days.** Its edges come from the commit counts; nothing else in either window is filtered.

## DRY notes

- **The alt text stays triplicated across the three cuts.** It is identical in `playgram.md`, `playgram.mini.md` and `playgram.nano.md`, and it is tempting to extract. Don't: the content pipeline reads each markdown file as an independent published artifact served raw at its own URL (`.claude/rules/content.md`), so a shared fragment would need an include mechanism the pipeline does not have and would break the property that the `.md` in the repo, on GitHub and on the site are the same bytes. Three coordinated edits is the correct cost.
- **The window dates are stated once per surface, and the surfaces are genuinely different audiences** — the SVG's visible labels, the SVG's `<desc>`, and the prose in two cuts. There is no shared runtime between an SVG served as an image and markdown compiled at build time, so a single source of truth for them would mean generating the SVG, which is the rejected regenerate-everything option, not a refactor.
- **No new helper, module or type is added.** The change is text and geometry inside one committed SVG plus prose in three markdown files. The x-scale arithmetic (`66 + day × 4.25`) is applied once, by hand, to six marks — extracting a function for six literals in a static asset would be worse than the literals.
- **The analysis scripts stay in `tmp/`.** The per-day classification used to pick the dates is throwaway: it reads a clone that is not part of this repository and cannot run in CI, and the dataset it would generate is explicitly not being adopted.
