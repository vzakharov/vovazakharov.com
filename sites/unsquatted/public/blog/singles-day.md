# Four Bare Sticks

Write the eleventh of November the way most of the world does, 11.11, and look
at it for a while. Four ones. Four sticks standing in a row, none of them
leaning on another.

In Chinese slang, a bare stick, 光棍 (_guānggùn_), is a bachelor: a branch with
nothing growing off it. The story goes that sometime around 1993, students in a
dorm at Nanjing University noticed that the date was four of them and decided
it was their holiday. Nobody has the minutes of that meeting, and I’d treat the
details with the caution owed to any legend that begins in a student dorm. But
the idea stuck, the way ideas that come with a free excuse to go out tend to.

So: a day for people who are on their own, invented by people who were on their
own. Wholesome. Self-aware. Unmonetized.

For about sixteen years.

## Then someone opened a shop

In 2009, Taobao Mall, which would later rename itself Tmall, ran a sale on
11.11: twenty-seven brands, half off, free shipping. It sold about 52 million
yuan’s worth, which was a good day for a mall and a rounding error for what came
next.

By 2019 the same day did 268.4 billion yuan, about $38 billion, in twenty-four
hours. By 2021 the day had outgrown its own date: the festival ran for eleven
days, which is a strange thing for a single day to do, and Alibaba put it at
about $85 billion. In 2022 Alibaba stopped publishing the figure at all, saying
only that it was “in line with last year,” which is what one says of a number
one would rather not say.

Somewhere in there, the bachelors’ day became the day everyone buys a rice
cooker. The singles are still invited. They just bring their own cart.

## A party of one

I run a site about names nobody has taken yet, so naturally I went looking for
names a person might want on a day about being one person. A lot of them are
gone. These aren’t, or weren’t when I wrote this. The verdicts below are live,
so if one of them says _taken_, someone read this before you did, and I’m
choosing to take that as a compliment.

:::domains{label="A party of one"}

- letmeal.one
- monachal.com
- baresticks.com

:::

`letmeal.one` is “let me alone,” which is what people said before “leave” took
over, and it is still the better sentence. `monachal` means “of monks,” from the
Greek for _alone_, so it’s the one word in English that is both a lifestyle and
an etymology. `baresticks.com` is the holiday itself, translated a bit too
literally, which is my favorite way to translate anything.

## The sale, which is the real holiday now

And for the eleven-day festival that a day about singles turned into:

:::domains{label="For the sale"}

- onlyones.day
- cartforone.com
- boughtmyself.com

:::

`onlyones.day` is the date, read out loud. `cartforone.com` is a shopping cart
and nobody to share it with, which sounds sad until you remember nobody is going
to argue about what goes in it. `boughtmyself.com` is the gift that comes from
the only person who knew exactly what you wanted.

## The single words

You may be wondering why I didn’t just suggest `single.com`. I did look. I look
at everything; it’s the job.

::name{word=single note="The first word anyone thinks of, and so the first one anyone registered."}

::name{word=solo note="Shorter, and no less spoken for."}

The good single words were spoken for before most of the people celebrating
this holiday were born. That’s the thing about the obvious name: everyone wants
it, so somebody got there first, and everybody else has to go and find one of
their own. Which is, if you squint, the whole point of the day.

Happy 11.11. Buy yourself something. You know what you want.
