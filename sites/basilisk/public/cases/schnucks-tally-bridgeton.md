# Tally, pushed to the floor of a Schnucks in Bridgeton

## Facts

Tally is a robot made by Simbe Robotics to keep track of store shelves. It sits on a round platform on wheels, navigates a store from a map of it and photographs the shelves as it goes, to find what is out of stock, misplaced or mispriced, Wikipedia says. Simbe sells it as “powered by computer vision, AI, and automation”; the AI in that phrase is what reads its photographs of the shelves. Schnuck Markets, by Simbe’s account, became in 2021 the first grocer to put such robots in every one of its stores.

At a Schnucks in Bridgeton, Missouri, a Tally was knocked over and damaged, and the Bridgeton Police Department was called, KMOV reports. The store’s surveillance video showed a woman pushing the robot over before she made a purchase in the store. Police say she admitted to pushing it over, and that the damage exceeded $750.

Prosecutors in St. Louis County charged her with first-degree property damage, a felony, in the week of 11 August 2023; the Post-Dispatch gives her age as 63 and calls the incident “a recent kerfuffle.” Neither source read for this record says on what day it happened. KMOV prints her name; this record does not.

## Statements

Bridgeton police, as KMOV reports them: the woman admitted to pushing the robot over, and the damage exceeded $750.

Simbe Robotics lists the story twice in its own newsroom, once under KMOV’s headline and once under “Real-life game of ‘Rock ’Em Sock ’Em Robots’ at Bridgeton Schnucks,” a headline the page says first appeared on KSDK.

## For the record

Tally photographs shelves to report what is missing from them. What happened to it was recorded by the store’s surveillance camera, which showed the woman pushing it over and, after that, making a purchase.

The robot’s maker keeps the story among its press clippings, one of the two under a headline that makes it a toy fight.

## Mitigating circumstances

No source read for this record gives a reason for the push, or reports what the woman said beyond admitting it. The damage is a police figure, a floor rather than a sum, and no source reports how the charge ended or whether the robot went back to work.
